Free tools for Claude, Gemini and other AI models

Good-looking 3D from an AI, in Blender.

Blender is a free program for making 3D pictures and animation. AI models can already drive it by writing code, but they have to build every light, surface and camera angle from nothing. Blender Studio gives the AI what a photographer has: lights, materials, a camera that frames the shot, and a way to look at its own picture and fix it. This page also tests, side by side, how much that helps.

Install it See it tested

These three were built and photographed using only the tools, from a script in the repository. Nothing was touched up by hand.

What it changes

Less to build from nothing.

Plain Blender

  • The AI writes Blender code line by line, from memory.
  • One command that this version of Blender does not have, and the step fails.
  • Lights, camera and materials all start from nothing, every time.
  • It only sees a picture if it sets up and runs a render itself.

With Blender Studio

  • 43 ready-made tools: "light this like a product shot", "make it ceramic", "frame it from three-quarters".
  • Everything is sized to the scene automatically, so a 10 cm mug and a 10 m room both come out right.
  • Every render comes back as a picture, with a warning if it is too dark or blown out.
  • A scene check catches floating objects, missing lights and subjects out of frame. Every step can be undone.

How a picture gets made

  1. BuildShapes, rocks, terrain, characters, or ready-made models.create_object · create_character
  2. FinishReal surfaces and softened edges, so objects catch the light.apply_material · add_polish
  3. StageA backdrop, a lighting rig, a framed camera and an overall mood.studio_lighting · frame_camera
  4. LookCheck the scene, render it, and study the picture.scene_check · render_preview
  5. FixChange what is wrong and render again, usually three to six rounds.undo · render_final

Blender starts by itself in the background. There is no add-on to install, no account and no API key, and it works on macOS, Windows and Linux with any AI app that supports MCP, the common way of plugging tools into AI apps.

The comparison

Same model, same request, both ways.

To see whether the tools help, the same model was given the same one-line request twice: once with plain Blender, where it writes code, renders and looks at the result, and once with Blender Studio. Thirteen such pairs were run, with Claude Haiku 4.5, Haiku 5.5, Sonnet 5.5 and Opus 5.5, and Gemini 3.6 Flash. Claude Opus 5.5 then judged each pair blind, under neutral file names.

The short version: Blender Studio's picture was judged better in 8 of 13 pairs, plain Blender's in 3, with two ties, and the average score was 5.6 against 4.9 out of 10. Nearly all of that gap comes from the oldest and weakest model. The stronger the model, the less the tools helped, and Claude Opus 5.5 did better without them.

What it shows

  • The weaker the model, the bigger the gain. Claude Haiku 4.5 went from 1.5 to 4.0 with the tools. Leaving it out, the other ten pairs were close to a draw: 5.9 plain against 6.0.
  • Haiku 4.5 was also cheaper and steadier with the tools. It cost $0.39 against $0.54, wrote 39% fewer tokens, and had 3 failed calls in 126 against 10 in 97. One of its plain runs spent 27 minutes, nearly all rendering.
  • Claude Opus 5.5 was better on its own. It averaged 7.7 with plain Blender and 6.8 with the tools, winning the mug and the robot and tying on the campsite.
  • Claude Sonnet 5.5 gained a little. Its average went from 5.5 to 6.7, at 2.4 times the cost and time.
  • Claude Haiku 5.5 came out even. It averaged 5.5 plain and 5.3 with the tools. The tools won on the mug and the campsite; plain Blender won on the robot.
  • The tools cost more to read. Claude models read 3.8 to 8.7 times as many tokens with them, because the descriptions of all 43 tools go with every request. In money the gap was modest: $1.11 against $0.94 for Opus, $0.04 against $0.03 for Haiku.
  • Time went both ways. The tools were slower for Haiku 5.5 and Sonnet. They were faster for Opus, whose plain runs chose slow, high-quality render settings: 1,314 s against 512 s over three requests.

What it does not show

  • One attempt per pair and three simple still-life requests. A repeat could move any single number a lot.
  • The judge, Claude Opus 5.5, also made six of the pictures. It saw every pair under neutral file names with no memory of making them, but it is still one model's taste.
  • Haiku 5.5 and Opus 5.5 were run on 13 October; Haiku 4.5, Sonnet and Gemini on 10 October, before one tool fault was fixed. That fault is what buried Haiku 4.5's mug under a huge blob of a handle.
  • Gemini has one pair, and the free daily allowance cut its Blender Studio run off after 8 of its 9 turns.
  • “Plain Blender” is this project's own three-tool stand-in, not another published package.
  • The test was run by the person who built the tools, not independently.

After the comparison

Fixing what held Opus back.

Opus 5.5 did worse with the tools than without, so I went through its recorded runs and the judge's comments to find out why, fixed what I found, and ran Opus again on the same three requests.

  • Loose framing. The camera measured a box around the subject, so round things came out small with dead space. It now measures the real shape.
  • Flat, washed-out staging. Glossy surfaces were too mirror-like and the backdrop was lit as brightly as the subject. Surfaces are softer, the backdrop now darkens as it recedes, and lighting has a contrast setting.
  • A guide that held it back. The design guide told the model to use few shapes, few materials and muted colours. It is now a set of tips that encourages detail and the model's own lights where those look better.
  • Wasted effort. A scene check raised false alarms, and materials had to be set one call at a time. Both are fixed, and the tool descriptions are 27% shorter.

The result: with the fixed tools Opus was judged better in all three pairs, averaging 8.3 against 7.2 for its plain-Blender pictures. Before the fixes it averaged 6.8 against 7.7. It also read half as many tokens as before (291k against 583k) and cost $1.00 against $1.11, close to the $0.94 of plain Blender.

Not tied to one AI

Gemini can drive it too.

Google Gemini was given a cut-down kit of 15 tools and asked for a toy rocket on a launch pad. It built the scene, rendered it nine times and revised between renders, with no failed steps.

It kept turning the lights up, which is why the reds came out pale. Nothing was corrected afterwards. The script that runs this fits inside Gemini's free daily allowance and is in the repository.

What is in the kit

43 tools, in plain terms.

Shapes

Basic solids, rocks, hilly ground, soft blobs, skins wrapped around stick skeletons, roughening, and cutting one shape out of another.

People

Stylised figures in toy, low-poly and blocky styles, realistic bodies, 11 poses and 6 motion clips such as walk, jump and dance.

Surfaces

24 materials including ceramic, gold, glass, wood, fabric and neon, plus photographed textures from Poly Haven.

Staging

Seven lighting rigs, a seamless studio backdrop, nine camera shots with automatic framing, and seven overall looks.

Seeing

Quick previews, a five-angle sheet for checking shape, a scene check for common mistakes, and full-quality final renders.

Motion and files

Turntables, camera moves, falling objects, draped cloth, MP4 video, and import or export for games, the web and 3D printing.

Install

Three steps, no account.

  1. Install Blender 4.2 or newer.
  2. Install uv, a small free program that fetches and runs the tools.
  3. Add the tools to your AI app.

    Claude Code:

    claude mcp add blender-studio -- uvx --from git+https://github.com/Just-Rice/blender-studio blender-studio-mcp

    Claude Desktop, Cursor and others, in the app's MCP settings file:

    {
  "mcpServers": {
    "blender-studio": {
      "command": "uvx",
      "args": ["--from", "git+https://github.com/Just-Rice/blender-studio", "blender-studio-mcp"]
    }
  }
}

Then ask for something, such as "Make a product shot of a ceramic mug on a warm backdrop."

Limits

  • No brush sculpting. Organic shapes come from noise, blobs, skins and cuts.
  • Realistic bodies have painted-on clothes and simple faces. They hold up at medium distance, not in close-up.
  • Motion clips are simple loops, not motion capture.
  • No fluid simulation.
  • Tested on macOS with Blender 5.2 only so far.

A word on safety

One of the tools lets the AI run code inside Blender, with the same access to your files as any program you run. Use it with AI apps you trust, and keep their approval prompts switched on.
