Free tools for Claude, Gemini and other AI models

Good-looking 3D from an AI, in Blender.

Blender is a free program for making 3D pictures and animation. AI models can already drive it by writing code, but they have to build every light, surface and camera angle from nothing. Blender Studio gives the AI what a photographer has: lights, materials, a camera that frames the shot, and a way to look at its own picture and fix it. In a blind test of four Claude models on eight requests, its pictures were judged better than plain Blender's in 21 of 32 pairs.

Install it See it tested

These three were built and photographed using only the tools, from a script in the repository. Nothing was touched up by hand.

What it changes

Less to build from nothing.

Plain Blender

  • The AI writes Blender code line by line, from memory.
  • One command that this version of Blender does not have, and the step fails.
  • Lights, camera and materials all start from nothing, every time.
  • It only sees a picture if it sets up and runs a render itself.

With Blender Studio

  • 43 ready-made tools: "light this like a product shot", "make it ceramic", "frame it from three-quarters".
  • Everything is sized to the scene automatically, so a 10 cm mug and a 10 m room both come out right.
  • Every render comes back as a picture, with a warning if it is too dark or blown out.
  • A scene check catches floating objects, missing lights and subjects out of frame. Every step can be undone.

How a picture gets made

  1. BuildShapes, rocks, terrain, characters, or ready-made models.create_object · create_character
  2. FinishReal surfaces and softened edges, so objects catch the light.apply_material · add_polish
  3. StageA backdrop, a lighting rig, a framed camera and an overall mood.studio_lighting · frame_camera
  4. LookCheck the scene, render it, and study the picture.scene_check · render_preview
  5. FixChange what is wrong and render again, usually three to six rounds.undo · render_final

Blender starts by itself in the background. There is no add-on to install, no account and no API key, and it works on macOS, Windows and Linux with any AI app that supports MCP, the common way of plugging tools into AI apps.

The comparison

Same model, same request, both ways.

To see whether the tools help, the same model was given the same one-line request twice: once with plain Blender, where it writes code, renders and looks at the result, and once with Blender Studio. This cycle covers Claude Haiku 4.5, Haiku 5.5, Sonnet 5.5 and Opus 5.5 on eight requests chosen to need people, physics, organic shapes, ready-made models, glass and video. Claude Opus 5.5 judged each pair blind, under neutral file names, and first ticked a short checklist of what each request asked for.

The short version: Blender Studio's picture was judged better in 21 of 32 pairs, plain Blender's in 7, with 4 ties. The average score was 5.6 against 4.3 out of 10, and the pictures met 81% of the requests' checklists against 73%. The weaker the model, the more the tools helped.

What it shows

  • The weaker the model, the bigger the gain. Haiku 4.5 went from 1.3 to 4.1 and won all eight pairs with the tools. Haiku 5.5 rose from 4.7 to 5.8 and Sonnet 5.5 from 4.8 to 5.9. Opus 5.5 was level, 6.5 against 6.6, winning 3, losing 4 and tying 1.
  • Requests got done more often. With the tools, pictures met 81% of their checklists against 73%. For Haiku 4.5 it was 62% against 14%: without the tools most of its pictures did not show what was asked.
  • Where plain Blender won. Opus without the tools made a rising hillside, a tidier mug shot, a cleaner cheese and a more natural spill. Haiku 5.5 and Sonnet lost the perfume bottle because their glass looked like plastic, and the hillside was the weakest request overall: tools won 1 of 4.
  • Fewer failed steps. 18 of 841 tool calls failed with the tools (2%), against 31 of 489 with plain Blender (6%).
  • Cost. Haiku 4.5 was cheaper with the tools ($1.09 against $1.61). The others paid a little more, mostly for reading the tool descriptions: Opus $3.43 against $3.20, Sonnet $0.67 against $0.47, Haiku 5.5 $0.13 against $0.08.

What it does not show

  • One attempt per pair. With 32 pairs the overall picture is clear, but any single pair can go either way on another day.
  • Opus judged its own pictures too, blind and with no memory of making them. Its scores for the same picture can drift by about half a point between sessions.
  • “Total time” is unreliable this cycle: the runs shared one laptop, which slept, swapped memory and had runs paused to keep it responsive. “Time on the model” is not affected.
  • Gemini was left out of this cycle: its free tier kept rejecting requests with “high demand” errors for minutes at a time.
  • “Plain Blender” is this project's own small stand-in, and the test was run by the person who built the tools.

What this cycle fixed, and whether it worked.

The judge's comments and the recorded runs pointed at three problems that hit several models. Each fix was then retested: every model ran the affected requests again with the fixed tools, and the judge compared the new picture with the old one, blind. A fix stays only if the new pictures win at least 60% of those comparisons and meet at least as much of the checklist.

  • Glass looked solid in previews. The fast preview renderer cannot see through one glass surface into another, so a bottle of perfume looked like dark plastic, and models replaced the glass with their own workarounds. Previews of scenes with glass now use the realistic renderer. Kept: the checklist went from 83% to 100% and the average from 5.5 to 6.4; Haiku 5.5 and Sonnet rose from 4.0 to 6.5 and 7.0. It won only 2 of 4, below the bar, because Haiku 4.5's and Opus's glass already looked right; kept as a judgement call.
  • A cloth could not go over one corner. The draping tool always centred the cloth on the whole table. It can now drop it on a named corner so it hangs over two edges. Kept: won 3 of 4 with 1 tie; checklist 50% to 69%.
  • Ground that rises like a hillside. The terrain tool only made gently rolling ground, and no model's hillside rose. A hill shape, rocky ledges and a soft cloud material were added. Reverted: every model used them, but the result was 1 win, 1 loss and 2 ties, and the checklist fell.
  • Smaller things. Rendering without a camera now sets one up instead of failing; common material names such as metal and cardboard work; a result Blender cannot send back no longer crashes it; and a render that runs out of graphics memory retries on the processor.

Not tied to one AI

Gemini can drive it too.

Google Gemini was given a cut-down kit of 15 tools and asked for a toy rocket on a launch pad. It built the scene, rendered it nine times and revised between renders, with no failed steps.

It kept turning the lights up, which is why the reds came out pale. Nothing was corrected afterwards. The script that runs this fits inside Gemini's free daily allowance and is in the repository.

Beyond still life

People, organic shapes, physics and video.

These come straight from the project's test suite, unretouched.

What is in the kit

43 tools, in plain terms.

Shapes

Basic solids, rocks, hilly ground, soft blobs, skins wrapped around stick skeletons, roughening, and cutting one shape out of another.

People

Stylised figures in toy, low-poly and blocky styles, realistic bodies, 11 poses and 6 motion clips such as walk, jump and dance.

Surfaces

24 materials including ceramic, gold, glass, wood, fabric and neon, plus photographed textures from Poly Haven.

Staging

Seven lighting rigs, a seamless studio backdrop, nine camera shots with automatic framing, and seven overall looks.

Seeing

Quick previews, a five-angle sheet for checking shape, a scene check for common mistakes, and full-quality final renders.

Motion and files

Turntables, camera moves, falling objects, draped cloth, MP4 video, and import or export for games, the web and 3D printing.

Install

Three steps, no account.

  1. Install Blender 4.2 or newer.
  2. Install uv, a small free program that fetches and runs the tools.
  3. Add the tools to your AI app.

    Claude Code:

    claude mcp add blender-studio -- uvx --from git+https://github.com/Just-Rice/blender-studio blender-studio-mcp

    Claude Desktop, Cursor and others, in the app's MCP settings file:

    {
  "mcpServers": {
    "blender-studio": {
      "command": "uvx",
      "args": ["--from", "git+https://github.com/Just-Rice/blender-studio", "blender-studio-mcp"]
    }
  }
}

Then ask for something, such as "Make a product shot of a ceramic mug on a warm backdrop."

Limits

  • No brush sculpting. Organic shapes come from noise, blobs, skins and cuts.
  • Realistic bodies have painted-on clothes and simple faces. They hold up at medium distance, not in close-up.
  • Motion clips are simple loops, not motion capture.
  • No fluid simulation.
  • Tested on macOS with Blender 5.2 only so far.

A word on safety

One of the tools lets the AI run code inside Blender, with the same access to your files as any program you run. Use it with AI apps you trust, and keep their approval prompts switched on.
